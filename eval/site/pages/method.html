Method

When comma publishes a new driving model on openpilot master or Hugging Face (commaai/openpilot_driving_models), our evaluation runner scores it on a fixed suite and publishes its results page. Each model is compared against lebowski (the July 2026 877M model that runs on the truck) and against the immediately preceding release.

The benchmark has two halves. The truck half runs recorded drives from a 2011 Mercedes G55 AMG in open-loop replay and closed-loop simulation; raw G55 drives stay private and their scores are published. The public half runs comma's public two-camera segments so anyone can reproduce those numbers from the published CSVs and code (below).

What each section measures

Public segments, open loop comma's public segments that have both road cameras, one segment as the unit, each car's own camera intrinsics. Segments where the car never moves are dropped. Public segments, closed loop The same kind of segments, run through comma's public worldmodel-4B with comma's ideal car, a vehicle that follows each command exactly. A replay of lebowski on these windows matches the truck's live records (correlation 0.99 to 1.000 on the lateral action). Speed and lateral error against what the truck did, and lateral gain: the model's lateral action over the truck's actual lateral motion, where 1 is right.
Truck, closed loop The truck windows, run through worldmodel-4B with the G55's measured steering response in place of the ideal car. Distance from the driven path at 2, 4 and 7.2 s, and the RMS lateral and longitudinal jerk of the requests. 98 windows from 13 drives and 5 camera setups.
Truck, recovery tests Closed loop after a disturbance: pushed 0.5 m sideways, or turned 2° off heading. A test behind a braking lead car runs on the 2 windows, from 1 drive, where a car ahead brakes. How far off the driven path the model is 2 and 4 s after the disturbance, and how hard it steers to recover.

Each section on a model page lists its own metrics, units, sample size and condition. A section that was not run for a model says so; it is never filled in from another model.

Unit of evidence and intervals

Every value is a mean over units: a 60 s segment in the public sections, a window (one stretch of one recorded drive) in the truck sections. Units are grouped by the drive they come from: comma's 39 moving public segments come from 23 drives, and the 28 closed-loop ones from 19. The interval beside a value is a 95% bootstrap over drives: resample whole drives with replacement, recompute the mean over all their units, repeat 4000 times, and keep the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Three adjacent minutes of one drive therefore count as one draw, not three.

With fewer than 10 drives there is no interval. In simulation, a nominal 95% interval of this kind covers the true mean about 90% of the time at 10 drives and 79% at 4, and less when the data are skewed. The G55 open loop comes from 12 drives, the closed loop from 13 and the recovery tests from 13, so they carry intervals. The braking-lead test comes from 1 drive, so it reports the mean, the median and win counts, including the number of drives in which the model did better, and no interval.

A delta compares a model with a baseline on the units both were scored on. It is model minus baseline, computed per unit and then bootstrapped the same way, so differences between cars, roads and light that affect both models cancel. The fraction beside it counts the units where the model did better; for lateral action rate and the lead of the lateral request, which have no better direction, it counts the units where the model's value was lower.

A blue dot or a red diamond means the delta's interval excludes zero (a grey triangle, for a measure with no better direction); a ring means the data do not separate the two models; a dash means there is no interval. Each page shows dozens of these comparisons with no correction for multiple tests, so a delta whose interval only just excludes zero is weak evidence. Where one segment dominates a mean, the median beside it shows it.

Two measures need care. The lead of the lateral request is the shift, searched from 0 to 1.5 s, at which the model's lateral request best correlates with the car's later lateral motion: a larger value means the request matches motion further ahead, so it comes earlier. Smoothing a request also moves that peak, so the lead is not a timing measure on its own and has no better direction. A value at either end of the search is only a bound and is left out, so its count can be below the section's. The plan speed error at 3 s reads the model's plan output, which does not separate models built on one frozen base: it is identical for the four October models on base f22e06e5.

Every public interval can be recomputed from the per-segment CSVs: uv run python -m geval.reproduce in eval/. The G55 score (0 to 100, higher is better) combines four G55 tests: closed-loop path keeping at 2 s (40%: 25% median and 15% mean distance from the recorded path), recovery at 2 s (25%: 12.5% after a 2° heading error, 12.5% after a 0.5 m lateral offset), open-loop lateral error across all scored models (20%) and closed-loop lateral jerk RMS (15%). Each part scales linearly from 0 (highest error among the scored models) to 100 (lowest error).

The site prints tiers, not places. Whole drives are resampled with replacement 2000 times, the same drives for every model, and every statistic, the scaling and the score are recomputed each time. A model sits in a higher tier only when it beat every model in the lower tiers in at least 95% of the resamples. Inside a tier the order changes from one resample to the next, so the leaderboard lists a tier newest first. Each single test gets its tiers by the same rule. rankings/tiers.json holds the tiers, each model's 95% rank interval and how often each model beat each other one. The site build runs uv run python -m geval.ranking --check site/_site, which fails if a page prints any other order: a place number, a medal, or a tier the resamples do not support.

Why open loop and closed loop can disagree

Open loop asks whether the model's output matches what a human did on a recorded drive. The output never changes the next frame, so a model can copy the human closely and still drift once its own steering moves the car. Closed loop feeds the steering back through a world model, and small errors build up.

In our 2026-10-27 ablation study, open-loop and closed-loop rankings diverged sharply. comma's recent models pair a frozen base policy with a reinforcement-learned residual head. With the residual head disabled, the frozen base achieved the lowest open-loop lateral error of any model on comma's public segments (0.074 m/s² vs lebowski's 0.109 m/s²), yet drifted 1.8 m off the driven path at 4 s in closed loop after a small yaw disturbance (where lebowski stayed within 0.4 m). Enabling the RL residual head pulled all nine closed-loop rollouts 1.2 to 1.3 m closer to the path while lowering open-loop lateral action rate by 33 to 40% and shifting lateral requests 0.13 to 0.26 s earlier. Open-loop imitation scoring counts both the earlier lead and the smoother steering profile as error against the human driver.

This matches published work: on the nuPlan benchmark, open-loop and closed-loop planning scores were negatively correlated (Dauner et al., 2023). The leaderboard weights closed-loop driving and recovery at 80% of the composite score (40% path keeping, 25% recovery, 15% closed-loop smoothness) and open-loop tracking at 20%, and shows every test side by side, each with its own tiers.

What the truck sections can and cannot show

They show how each model behaves with a camera height, a vehicle and a steering response that comma's fleet does not cover, and whether a ranking on comma's cars holds on a vehicle unlike them.

  • They come from one truck. Nothing in them says how a model drives another vehicle.
  • The windows come from 12 to 14 drives of one truck. Each section shows its count, and the conditions (day, night, speed, camera setup) are not balanced.
  • Others cannot reproduce them: the drives are private, and only aggregate scores and a few checked stills are published.
  • Closed loop is a simulation. The world model is weakest at night, rolls out 7.2 s at most, and pulls late frames toward what was actually recorded, so differences that appear only past about 4 s carry little weight. In a separate test it showed 0 to 20% of an added lateral offset 3 to 4 s after it, so the front-page ranking uses 2 s.
  • No newer model has steered the truck. It drives on lebowski, and every other model is scored by replay.

Stills are a few camera frames per condition with the model's path drawn on them, each checked so that nothing in it identifies the truck, its owner or the place.
