What was asked

The yaw-rate sensor reads a small non-zero value at standstill, and every sideways driving label integrates it (earlier note). Does a truck adapter trained on labels with that offset removed drive closer to the human driver?

What was done

  • The shipped training recipe was run four times: shipped labels and corrected labels, two random seeds each. Nothing else changed.
  • Open loop scoring on nine drives recorded after everything else and never used for anything, plus the 17 validation drives.
  • Primary measure: the distance between the model's planned path and the driver's actual path 4 s ahead, per drive, with a 95% interval resampled by drive.
  • Pass rule, fixed before the run: a change below zero with an interval excluding zero on the new drives; the same sign on validation for both seeds; and a change larger than the difference between two seeds trained on the same labels.
  • A re-run of the shipped recipe was checked against the shipped adapter and reproduced it exactly.

Result

The rule fails. Change in the 4 s distance to the driver's path, corrected labels against shipped labels (negative is better):

9 new drives-0.004 [-0.012, +0.005]mean error 0.49 m; better on 5 of 9

17 validation drives, seed 2-0.012 [-0.016, -0.007]

  • The new-drive interval includes zero. On validation the change is largest above 20 m/s, at -0.02 to -0.03 m.
  • The change from labels is smaller than the difference between two seeds, so the third part of the rule fails.
  • Scored against the labels as recorded, without the correction applied to the reference, the change is nil: -0.002 m [-0.007, +0.004] on the new drives.

What did change. The model's sideways command on straight road lost a small standing bias. On the new drives it went from about -0.016 to -0.009 m/s2, and on validation from -0.009 to about 0, for both seeds, with intervals excluding zero.

The offset is real and the adapter does learn it. At this training size the effect on the plan is millimetres, below the spread between drives and below seed noise.

The choice of random seed moved the 4 s number by about 4 percent, more than the thing being tested. Single-seed comparisons of this adapter need care.

What it does not show

  • Whether the correction matters with more motorway driving. The effect grows with speed and the new drives were mostly slow.
  • Whether the standstill reading is the right correction while moving.
  • Anything in closed loop.
  • Anything beyond one truck, one driver and one adapter recipe.
