What was asked

The push bench in simulation behaved differently on roads with a centre line and no painted right line (see the push bench by lane lines note). That was a simulator observation. The question here needs no simulator: on real recorded frames, on two-way roads where the model sees a centre line and no right line, does its planned path sit to one side of the path the driver then drove, more than it does on roads with both lines?

The test was written down before the result was read. It counts as a signal if the centre-line-only offset at 4 s is 10 cm or more to one side, its interval over drives excludes zero, and it differs from the both-lines offset with an interval that also excludes zero. If not, the question is closed as "no line-type offset in the plan".

What was done

  • Open loop on 17 held-out drives of one truck and one driver, real camera frames, stock model and the truck's camera adapter. No training, no simulator.
  • Road class for each step comes from the model's own confidence for the nearest line on each side, called seen at 0.5 or more.
  • Measure: the plan minus the driver's path, sideways, at 4 s ahead (also 2 s). Positive is toward the centre line.
  • Straight driving only: the driver's lateral acceleration under 0.3 m/s² now and 0.5 s later, speed 5 m/s and over. This kept 50,570 of 74,032 steps.
  • Means per class with a 95% interval resampled by drive.

Paired by drive, for the adapter, the centre-line-only offset minus the both-lines offset is 0.000 m [-0.055, +0.064] at 4 s over 15 drives (7 positive), and -0.009 m at 2 s. The pass rule (10 cm, interval excluding zero) is not met. On roads with a centre line and no painted right line the plan sits where the driver drove, to within about 4 cm, for both models.

Two other cells are not about this question. The stock model sits 11 cm to the right on two-line roads at speed, which is the camera-position effect the adapter was built to remove. The "right line only" class showed +0.23 m for the adapter, but it is 397 steps on 7 drives, under 1% of the data.

Closed: there is no line-type offset in the plan to train against. The bench's line-type behaviour under a push stays a description of the bench.

What it does not show

  • Where the lane centre is. The reference is where this driver drove, and a driver may keep to one side of a centre line by choice.
  • Anything in closed loop.
  • Anything beyond 17 drives of one truck and one driver, with road class taken from the model's own line confidences and not from a map.
