What was asked

The driving model has an input for the driver's stated intent: turn left, turn right, lane change left, lane change right. openpilot sets it from the turn signal. The truck's stack has always fed that input zeros, and so has every evaluation on this site, although the truck's turn signal is on the CAN bus and decoded. The question: if the model is told when the signal comes on, does its plan follow the driver's manoeuvre more closely?

The test and its pass rule were written before the run. The result counts as a signal if the plan's sideways gap to the driver's path at 4 s falls by 10% or more after a signal onset, the interval over drives excludes zero, and the steps with no signal do not get worse by more than 1 cm. A second set of drives was to repeat it before it went anywhere near the truck.

What was done

  • Open loop on real recorded camera frames of one truck and one driver. No simulator, no training, nothing deployed.
  • At each recorded turn-signal onset, one pulse is written to the intent input for one 0.2 s step. The model keeps its own 6.6 s history of that input. Two arms were run against a zeros capture: a lane-change pulse on the signalled side at every onset, and a turn pulse under 9 m/s with a lane-change pulse at 9 m/s and over (9 m/s is openpilot's minimum speed for a lane change).
  • Measure: the sideways gap between the planned path and the path the driver then drove, at 2 s and 4 s ahead, over the 6 s after each onset while the signal is on, paired by step against zeros, with a 95% interval resampled by drive.
  • The patched script with no pulses reproduces the unpatched capture exactly (74,032 steps, largest plan and action difference 0.000000).
  • First set: 17 held-out drives, 134 signal onsets. Second set: 6 other drives (three trips) recorded later, 37 onsets, scored once with the same script.
  • Third set, a second confirmation built to outside reviewers' specification, with the rule and scorer written before its drives were captured: the stock model with no adapter (so no drive is one the adapter trained on), one lane-change pulse on the signalled side at a signal onset at 9 m/s and over, nothing under 9 m/s, no second pulse on the same side within 7 s, pulse placement taken from the evaluation's own frame grid. One signal event is one unit, scored over every step from 0 to 6 s after the onset whether or not the signal is still on, and needs 5 or more scored steps. The planned pass: the per-event change in the gap at 4 s is below zero with the by-drive interval excluding zero on 8 or more drives, still so with the drive with most events left out, and the steering command not worse. It was drawn from 20 drives not read for this question; 3 had no usable signal recording or driver path, so 17 were captured and 14 of them have events.

  • On the first set the gap at 4 s is lower on 13 of 15 drives. Without the signal the plan sits 0.52 m short of the driver's path on the signalled side at 4 s (0.82 m at 9 m/s and over); with the pulse it is 0.13 m past (0.04 m at 9 m/s and over). All other steps are unchanged on average.
  • The effect is in the first 2 s after onset (1.244 to 0.691 m at 4 s) and mostly gone by 6 s. Under 9 m/s, which is junction turns, a lane-change pulse changes nothing and a turn pulse makes the gap larger (1.693 to 1.832 m).
  • Not confirmed on a second set. By the rule written in advance the second set fails: the interval includes zero and three of the six drives are lower, three higher. One drive holds 27 of the 37 onsets and supplies all of the gain (1.217 to 0.944 m). The other five drives pooled get larger, about 1.28 to 1.44 m over 181 steps.
  • A per-event reading made after the fact (not the planned measure, thresholds set after looking) shows the same pattern: 72 of 97 events lower by 2 cm or more on the first set, 19 of 30 on the second. The largest increases are on the second set: a 7 m/s turn where the pulsed plan goes about 2 m past the driver's path, and a 12 m/s event where it goes 1.1 m past. A lane-change pulse at every onset is too blunt.
  • Is the pulse just a sideways shift toward the signalled side? A single fixed shift fitted on the first set at 9 m/s and over recovers 37% of the pulse's gain there (gap 1.028 with zeros, 0.861 with the shift, 0.575 with the pulse), and on the second set is 0.30 m above the pulse (1.085 against 0.781). The size of the pulse's change tracks how far the driver still has to move (correlation 0.63 and 0.44), so the model is sizing the move from the picture.

One drive holds 24 of the 47 events, and the mean of the 14 per-drive means is -0.04 m (8 drives lower, 6 higher). The 14 drives are about 9 or 10 separate trips, so by-drive intervals are too narrow. The result is closed as written: feeding the turn signal improves one lane-change-heavy drive. On the other drives it is indistinguishable from zero, with one large overshoot on a turn.
  • Scorer correction after outside review. The first scoring window left out the pulse step. With it included the plan change is -0.22 m [-0.32, +0.11], and -0.06 m [-0.23, +0.19] without the largest drive. The gap, better-or-worse and 2 s figures from the first scorer are no longer shown because they were not rescored. After a gap in the camera frames, an onset inside the gap is pulsed late or missed.
  • The steering command improves, with the interval excluding zero both ways. Checked at three lags of the driver's lateral acceleration (0.2, 0.5 and 1.0 s), the change is -0.12, -0.12 and -0.11 m/s², each interval excluding zero. Left and right events are balanced (24 and 23). It was a guard in the plan and not the test, so it is a lead only, and nothing is built on it.
  • The fed plan moves further toward the signalled side than the driver did: event means at 4 s are 1.26 m for the driver, 0.90 m with zeros and 1.43 m with the pulse. The worst event is a 4.3 m move by the driver where zeros already planned 5.4 m and the pulse made it 7.6 m.
  • The pulse helps on lane changes and overshoots on turns and exits. Splitting events by how far the driver had moved toward the signalled side, measured in the truck's frame at each step, the gap to the driver at 4 s is: lane changes, driver -0.93 m, zero-intent plan -1.30 m, fed plan -0.77 m; turns and exits, driver +4.73 m, zero-intent plan +4.33 m, fed plan +4.91 m. A speed gate does not separate the two, since the worst events are at 9 to 16 m/s. This split was made after the score was read and selects on what the driver did next, so it is description only. An earlier reading here, that most of the gain sat where the driver hardly moved, came from the same binning: during a lane change the truck soon points along the new lane, so that offset is small or negative.
  • Steps outside every 7 s window: 0.1 to 0.3% differ at all, and the mean error is unchanged to the third decimal.

What it does not show

  • That this works in general. The first set is a measured effect; the second does not repeat it by the rule set in advance, and the third, built to a stricter specification on other drives and a different model setting, fails its rule too. Its events are few and uneven across drives. The drives available for this question have now all been read.
  • Closed-loop behaviour, which was not tested and cannot be on the simulator, which has no lane changes. The steering command was scored only in the third set, as a guard.
  • Steps after a signal is cancelled while the pulse is still in the model's memory. The scoring window keeps only steps where the signal is on, so those fall into the unchanged-steps row, which could not have shown them (about 1.6% of those steps differ between the arms). Some signals were pulsed twice (171 pulses for 141 events), which openpilot would not do. Pulse placement is off on 3 to 4% of steps.
  • How the model's intent input was labelled in training; that is not public. openpilot sets the lane-change intent when the driver also nudges the wheel, not at signal onset, and the truck has no steering-torque signal to copy that rule.
  • An improvement to the model. This is an input used as its makers intended, which the truck's stack never connected.

State: closed. The pulse improves one lane-change-heavy drive and is indistinguishable from zero on the others, with one large overshoot on a turn. The one open lead is the steering command's lower error, which would need its own test written in advance, on new drives. No such drives are being collected for it.
