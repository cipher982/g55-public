What was asked

N16 is comma's driving model (96244b1f_12800, "N16 nav"). In bench8, the closed-loop simulator built here (the model steers a simulated truck while the camera view is redrawn from recorded frames), N16 often fails to recover from sideways pushes. A "push" moves the simulated truck 1.0 or 1.5 m sideways off its recorded path, and "recovery" is the share of pushes the truck returns from. The question was whether a small steering correction (a residual) trained on top of a frozen N16 helps.

What was done

Baseline first: on 18 lane windows (36 episodes per push size), N16 recovered 0.36 of 1.0 m pushes and 0.25 of 1.5 m pushes, and 0 of 18 night episodes at 1.5 m, failing mostly by overshoot (1.0 m) or a wrong-way first response (1.5 m). Another driving model, lebowski, recovered 0.64 and 0.47.

  • First residual (a small network adding up to 2 m/s² of lateral action), trained with DAgger (rollouts in the simulator labelled by a controller that knows the true pose). Fewer lane departures and no more jerk, but both paired intervals reached zero and it read worse on live-drive hard moments.
  • Round 2 added inputs: +0.04 and +0.06 on 63 locked-test day windows (intervals include zero), worse than N16 at 1.5 m on a fresh evening.
  • The "x3 start": N16's residual output written three times as wide, untrained. It already recovered more often than N16, and departed the lane less (dev windows, 135: 1.0 m 0.18 to 0.07, 1.5 m 0.39 to 0.14; locked test day windows, 63: recovery 0.60 to 0.71 and 0.40 to 0.48).
  • TD3 (a reinforcement-learning method) started from the x3 start. Pilot 1 collapsed by epoch 24: the critic barely distinguished lateral actions and the actor drifted into a constant rightward shift (unpushed departures 0.04 to 0.83). Pilot 2 added correlated exploration noise, an anchor to the starting action, and a check that the critic had learned before the actor trained. It did not collapse; gains on windows it trained on (recovery 0.70 to 0.84 and 0.54 to 0.80) held after export to bench8.
  • A 201-epoch run, three seeds, scored on 44 held-out windows.

In the long run every seed's interval over drives cleared zero against its own unpushed run. Wrong-way first turns at 1.5 m fell from 13 to 6-7 of 270 pushes, and lateral jerk dropped. On the 91 training windows the gain against the driver's path was +0.19 and +0.29, so the line was learned where it trained; recovery itself transferred. Training toward the lane centre instead did not change the held-out result measurably.

What it does not show

  • Held-out intervals resample 3 drives. The locked test, the learned truck plant and hard moments were not run for the long run, and nothing has gone to the truck.
  • Which of pilot 2's four changes prevented the collapse was not separated.
  • Simulation on recorded drives; the later stages use day windows only.
