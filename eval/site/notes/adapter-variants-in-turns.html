What was asked

An earlier open-loop study of the truck's camera adapter found that the shipped adapter lowers the turn gain (the plan's lateral acceleration over the driver's) a little: 0.853 for the stock model, 0.829 for the shipped adapter, 0.872 for a variant trained on lane paint with a teacher and without the driver's path labels. It left open whether that matters when the model drives. In closed loop, where does each of the three sit in turns?

What was done

The turn measure from the delay-request note was applied to the stock model and to the variant (lane paint + teacher, trained on 4 drives), on the same closed-loop bench, settings and 30 validation windows, one seed, from per-step traces. The measure is the offset to the outside of the driver's recorded path, on the steps where the shipped adapter's unpushed run commands 0.7 m/s² or more. 9 of the 30 windows, from 3 drives, have such steps. Differences are against the shipped adapter, paired by window, with 95% intervals over windows. A third variant (lane paint + teacher + pose) was not run.

The shipped adapter does not run wider than the stock model in turns; the interval of the difference spans zero. The variant with the highest open-loop turn gain runs widest, 15 cm further out than the shipped adapter, and is wider on all nine windows. So the open-loop turn gain did not predict closed-loop turn tracking here. The driver's path labels in the shipped recipe, which the variant lacks, are what keep the truck on the driver's line in turns. An earlier closed-loop score could not separate these variants on recovery; this measure does.

What it does not show

  • Scale: nine windows from three drives, one seed, a simulated vehicle.
  • Anything about the lane centre. The reference is the driver's path, and a variant trained toward that path is expected to sit closer to it.
  • Any change on the truck: nothing is deployed, and the shipped adapter stays.
  • Outside review. None, because the result confirms the shipped choice and claims no improvement.
