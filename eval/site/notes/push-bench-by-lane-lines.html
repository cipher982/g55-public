What was asked

In the closed-loop push bench, some pushed episodes are not back on the path by 4 s. Do those episodes share a road type, as seen through the model's own lane-line confidences?

What was done

No new run. The per-step trace of the adapter at its current settings (simulated vehicle, 30 validation windows from 6 recorded drives of one truck and one driver, yaw pushes of 1.0 m and 1.5 m to each side) was read again with the model's lane-line confidences. A window's class comes from its unpushed run: the median confidence, 2 to 4 s in, of the line to the left of the truck and of the line to the right, each called seen at 0.5 or more. The classes:

  • lines on both sides: 17 windows (median speed 22 m/s);
  • a line on the left only: 12 windows (median 17 m/s, four of them under 10 m/s). Stills from three of these show daylight two-way roads with a double yellow centre line and a curb, and no painted right line. On a two-way road the left is the centre-line side;
  • neither: 1 window, not tabulated.

By the confidence in both lines instead of by class: where it is 0.9 or more (56 episodes, 14 windows, median 26 m/s) the median distance from the unpushed run is 0.25 m at 4 s and 0.09 m at 5.6 s. Where it is under 0.1 (28 episodes, 7 windows, median 16.5 m/s) it is 0.67 m at 4 s and 0.48 m at 5.6 s.

In the median episode, a push to the left is back within 5 cm of the unpushed run by 4 s on both road classes. A push to the right displaces the truck less while it is applied (0.34 to 0.37 m against 0.52 to 0.60 m for the 1.0 m push) and is followed by a swing past the unpushed run to the left: about a quarter of a metre where both lines are seen, 0.53 to 0.60 m where only the centre line is, shrinking to 0.1 to 0.2 m by 5.6 s.

What it does not show

  • The tail. These are medians. The episodes that keep diverging are a different set, mostly slow windows.
  • That road type is the cause. Road class is confounded with speed and with curvature.
  • Scale: 12 and 17 windows from 6 drives, one seed, a simulated vehicle, no training, nothing deployed. The class comes from the adapter's own confidences.
  • How close the truck came to a line. The model's reading of the centre line's distance moved by only 0.06 to 0.56 of the truck's true offset in these steps, so it is not a measurement of position.
  • That the asymmetry between left and right pushes is real driving behaviour. It may come from the bench: earlier work here found the unpushed runs sit left of the recorded path because the models put the lane centre there, and the views after a push are synthesized.
  • Outside review. None, because nothing is claimed from it.

What it changes: a recovery share from this bench mixes two road types and two push directions that behave differently, so those should be reported apart.
